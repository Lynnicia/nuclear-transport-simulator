    How the model works

    Proteins larger than about 40 kDa can't slip through the nuclear pore's mesh of FG-repeat proteins. Transport receptors (karyopherins) can, because they bind those FG repeats. Small solutes pass on their own. Free Ran can't cross in this model: it moves only as part of a complex.

    Import: importin binds cargo carrying a nuclear localization signal (NLS) wherever it meets it. Importin carrying cargo can enter the nucleus but can't leave with it. In the nucleus, Ran-GTP binds importin and forces the cargo off. The importin–Ran-GTP complex leaves, and in the cytoplasm Ran-GAP hydrolyzes the GTP, freeing importin to go again.

    Export: exportin binds nuclear export signal (NES) cargo only when it also holds Ran-GTP. That three-part complex exits, Ran-GAP hydrolyzes the GTP, and the cargo is released in the cytoplasm.

    The gradient: RCC1 makes Ran-GTP in the nucleus and Ran-GAP destroys it in the cytoplasm. NTF2 picks up Ran-GDP in the cytoplasm and carries it through the pore. In the nucleus RCC1 swaps GDP for GTP; Ran-GTP doesn't bind NTF2, so NTF2 is released to make another trip. That asymmetry sets the direction of both import and export. The energy comes from GTP hydrolysis.

    Things to try

    1. Run the normal cell until the chart levels off. Do NLS cargo and NES cargo end up above or below the dashed even-mixing line? How does the small solute compare?
    2. Set nuclear pores to 0. What still happens and what stops?
    3. Turn off RCC1. Predict what happens to the Ran-GTP line first, then to each cargo. Explain the order.
    4. With RCC1 off, where does importin + cargo get stuck? Why can't it return to the cytoplasm?
    5. Reset with NTF2 at 0. Where does Ran end up, and what happens to import and export?
    6. Turn off Ran-GAP instead. Where does importin pile up, and why does import stall?
    7. Add leptomycin B. Which cargo is affected? Then try mutating the NLS.
    8. Start with cargo in the wrong compartments. Can the cell fix it? Watch the GTP counter: what does sorting cost?
    9. Reset with importin at 0, then with 5. How does the number of receptors change the rate of import?

    Simplifications: molecules move by random diffusion in 2-D; reactions happen on contact with set probabilities; RanBP1 is folded into Ran-GAP, and importin-α/β is one player. Rates are scaled for viewing, not measured values.
